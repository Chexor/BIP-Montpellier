# Brainstorm - Health & Aging

## Working Lens

Aim for a demo that is concrete, humane, and testable in a few minutes. The strongest hackathon ideas in health and aging usually avoid claiming to diagnose or replace care. They help people notice, decide, coordinate, remember, prepare, or ask for help.

## Idea Directions

### 1. Medication and Routine Companion

- Problem: Older adults often have complex medication, appointment, and hydration routines.
- Demo: A simple daily dashboard with reminders, confirmations, caregiver visibility, and missed-routine escalation.
- Data needed: Manual schedule, optional sample medication list.
- Strength: Easy to demo and immediately understandable.
- Risk: Needs careful privacy and non-medical wording.

### 2. Fall Risk Home Walkthrough

- Problem: Homes gradually become unsafe as mobility changes.
- Demo: Upload or select photos of rooms, then produce a safety checklist for lighting, cables, rugs, stairs, bathroom access, and emergency access.
- Data needed: Room checklist or example photos.
- Strength: Practical prevention focus.
- Risk: Avoid overclaiming computer vision accuracy.

### 3. Care Circle Coordination

- Problem: Family members, neighbors, nurses, and volunteers often duplicate work or miss tasks.
- Demo: Shared board for visits, groceries, medication pickup, appointments, mood notes, and urgent needs.
- Data needed: Mock care network.
- Strength: Social and logistical, not clinically risky.
- Risk: Needs a very clear core workflow.

### 4. Loneliness Signal and Activity Matcher

- Problem: Social isolation is a major aging-health risk.
- Demo: Lightweight weekly check-in that suggests local activities, calls, clubs, or visits based on energy level, mobility, language, and interests.
- Data needed: Sample activity catalog.
- Strength: Human-centered and positive.
- Risk: Needs sensitivity around mood and mental health.

### 5. Discharge-to-Home Helper

- Problem: After hospital discharge, patients and families often misunderstand follow-up steps.
- Demo: Convert discharge instructions into a plain-language plan with appointments, red flags, tasks, questions for the doctor, and caregiver handoff.
- Data needed: Sample discharge document.
- Strength: Strong impact, clear demo.
- Risk: Medical safety boundaries must be explicit.

### 6. Mobility-Friendly City Route Planner

- Problem: Older adults may avoid activities because routes are physically uncertain.
- Demo: Route scoring based on benches, shade, slope, toilets, crossings, tram stops, and walking distance.
- Data needed: Map sample or manual points of interest.
- Strength: Good fit if the hackathon has local Montpellier data.
- Risk: Data availability may limit realism.

### 7. Cognitive Load Reducer for Admin Tasks

- Problem: Health, insurance, and municipal paperwork can be hard to understand.
- Demo: Upload a form or letter and get a plain-language summary, required actions, deadlines, and a call script.
- Data needed: Sample forms or letters.
- Strength: Useful across many aging contexts.
- Risk: Needs privacy framing and document limitations.

### 8. Remote Check-In Without Surveillance

- Problem: Caregivers want reassurance, but older adults may dislike invasive monitoring.
- Demo: Consent-based check-in using gentle signals: kettle use, daily button tap, calendar activity, or optional message reply.
- Data needed: Simulated sensor or manual events.
- Strength: Strong ethical angle.
- Risk: Hardware is hard unless simulated.

### 9. Appointment Companion

- Problem: Older adults may forget what to ask during medical appointments and may struggle to remember what was said afterward.
- Demo: Before-visit question builder, during-visit note capture, and after-visit summary with next steps.
- Data needed: Mock appointment types and sample notes.
- Strength: Very practical and easy to role-play.
- Risk: Needs careful handling of sensitive health information.

### 10. Nutrition Fridge Planner

- Problem: Aging, illness, medication, and mobility can make meal planning harder.
- Demo: Suggest simple meals from available ingredients, dietary restrictions, chewing difficulty, budget, and cooking energy.
- Data needed: Small recipe dataset.
- Strength: Friendly, concrete, and easy to demo.
- Risk: Avoid clinical nutrition claims unless using approved guidance.

### 11. Voice-First Daily Assistant

- Problem: Many digital health tools assume good eyesight, dexterity, and confidence with apps.
- Demo: Voice interface for reminders, check-ins, appointments, simple questions, and caregiver messages.
- Data needed: Scripted voice flows.
- Strength: Accessibility angle is strong.
- Risk: Voice tech can be fragile in noisy demo environments.

### 12. Early Frailty Pattern Dashboard

- Problem: Small changes in activity, sleep, appetite, and mood can be missed until they become serious.
- Demo: Simulated weekly trends with gentle flags for "worth checking in" rather than diagnosis.
- Data needed: Synthetic time-series data.
- Strength: Good for dashboards and storytelling.
- Risk: Must avoid implying medical prediction without validation.

### 13. Pharmacy Pickup Optimizer

- Problem: Medication pickup, refills, substitutions, and caregiver availability can become a coordination burden.
- Demo: Refill calendar, pickup delegation, low-stock warnings, and pharmacy call checklist.
- Data needed: Mock prescriptions and pharmacy hours.
- Strength: Narrow scope, high everyday usefulness.
- Risk: Real pharmacy integration is out of scope for a hackathon.

### 14. Age-Friendly Event Finder

- Problem: Activities are often listed without accessibility details that older adults need.
- Demo: Filter events by seating, noise level, transport access, toilets, cost, language, companionship, and mobility requirements.
- Data needed: Sample local event list.
- Strength: Strong social-health story.
- Risk: Depends on local data unless mocked.

### 15. Caregiver Burnout Radar

- Problem: Informal caregivers often become exhausted before they ask for help.
- Demo: Weekly caregiver check-in that suggests task redistribution, respite options, or a message to the care circle.
- Data needed: Mock caregiver profile and task load.
- Strength: Addresses the support network, not only the patient.
- Risk: Needs empathetic wording and no mental-health overclaims.

### 16. Smart Emergency Card

- Problem: In an emergency, key medical and contact information is often scattered or unavailable.
- Demo: Printable and phone-friendly emergency card with medications, allergies, conditions, contacts, doctor, language needs, and QR access.
- Data needed: Manual profile form.
- Strength: Very buildable and demo-friendly.
- Risk: Security and consent need to be considered.

### 17. Rehabilitation Exercise Buddy

- Problem: People may stop doing prescribed rehab exercises because instructions are unclear or motivation drops.
- Demo: Visual schedule, safe reminders, progress tracking, and "ask your therapist" prompts when pain or uncertainty appears.
- Data needed: Mock exercise plan.
- Strength: Good interaction design opportunity.
- Risk: Must not prescribe exercises independently.

### 18. Memory-Friendly Photo Journal

- Problem: People with mild cognitive impairment and their families may benefit from structured, low-pressure memory support.
- Demo: Daily photo, names, places, short captions, and family prompts that build a gentle timeline.
- Data needed: Sample photos and profiles.
- Strength: Emotionally compelling.
- Risk: Privacy and dignity are central.

### 19. Benefits and Services Navigator

- Problem: Older adults and caregivers may not know which local services, subsidies, transport, meal help, or home adaptations exist.
- Demo: Guided questionnaire that produces a personalized list of services and next actions.
- Data needed: Small service directory.
- Strength: Strong public-sector fit.
- Risk: Needs accurate local source data.

### 20. Inclusive Waiting Room Companion

- Problem: Clinic visits can be stressful because waiting times, instructions, and next steps are unclear.
- Demo: Patient-facing flow that explains where to go, what to prepare, estimated wait, and accessible assistance options.
- Data needed: Mock clinic flow.
- Strength: Good if the hackathon involves hospitals or patient experience.
- Risk: Real integration is complex.

### 21. Polypharmacy Risk Conversation Starter

- Problem: Older adults taking many medications may not know when to ask for a medication review.
- Demo: Non-diagnostic checklist that identifies questions to raise with a pharmacist or doctor.
- Data needed: Sample medication profiles.
- Strength: High relevance in aging.
- Risk: Needs strict "conversation starter" framing.

### 22. End-of-Life Wishes Organizer

- Problem: Families often do not know preferences around care, contacts, documents, and rituals until a crisis.
- Demo: Sensitive guided organizer for values, emergency contacts, documents, and conversation prompts.
- Data needed: Manual questionnaire.
- Strength: Meaningful and under-addressed.
- Risk: Heavy topic; may not fit all hackathon audiences.

### 23. Intergenerational Skill Exchange

- Problem: Older adults may want social connection and practical help without feeling like passive recipients of care.
- Demo: Match older adults and younger volunteers for language practice, digital help, cooking, local history, errands, or tutoring.
- Data needed: Mock participant profiles.
- Strength: Positive aging narrative.
- Risk: Safety, trust, and moderation matter.

### 24. Accessible Medication Label Reader

- Problem: Small print and confusing labels make medication use harder.
- Demo: Camera-based label capture that reads text aloud, highlights dose timing, and turns it into reminders.
- Data needed: Sample label images.
- Strength: Clear accessibility value.
- Risk: OCR errors can be dangerous, so verification is essential.

### 25. Heatwave Safety Check

- Problem: Older adults are at higher risk during heatwaves, especially when isolated or without cooling.
- Demo: Weather-aware checklist, hydration prompts, cool-place map, and caregiver check-in trigger.
- Data needed: Weather data or mocked forecast, local cooling locations.
- Strength: Timely, climate-health angle.
- Risk: Local data quality may vary.

### 26. Sleep and Night Safety Helper

- Problem: Night-time bathroom trips and poor sleep can increase fall risk and caregiver concern.
- Demo: Routine planner for lighting, hydration timing, bathroom path checklist, and morning check-in.
- Data needed: Manual home profile.
- Strength: Narrow, preventive, and easy to prototype.
- Risk: Avoid diagnosing sleep disorders.

### 27. Digital Confidence Coach

- Problem: Health services increasingly require portals, QR codes, apps, and online forms.
- Demo: Step-by-step assistant for booking appointments, using patient portals, downloading certificates, or joining teleconsultations.
- Data needed: Mock portal flows.
- Strength: Very relevant and humane.
- Risk: Avoid collecting real credentials.

### 28. Shared Symptom Timeline

- Problem: Symptoms, medication changes, falls, and appointments are often remembered inconsistently.
- Demo: Timeline for older adult and caregiver to record events, then generate a concise doctor visit summary.
- Data needed: Mock health events.
- Strength: Clinician-friendly output.
- Risk: Must distinguish observations from conclusions.

## Evaluation Criteria for Choosing

- Can we demo the core value in under three minutes?
- Can we avoid medical diagnosis or unsafe advice?
- Can we build a credible prototype with available data?
- Does the idea help older adults, caregivers, or professionals directly?
- Is there a clear before-and-after improvement?

## Pitch Framing

- Problem: Aging creates more care complexity while support networks are stretched.
- Insight: Many failures are coordination, comprehension, and environment failures, not only medical failures.
- Solution angle: Make the next safe action obvious for the older adult and their support circle.
- Guardrail: Support decisions and coordination; do not replace clinicians.
